Uniqueness---1AC

Reshoring is stalling NOW. Data centers are outcompeting factories for grid capacity, and transformer lead times alone can disqualify a plant.

Segers 26 [Alexandra; July 9, 2026; General Manager, Tochi Advisors, 15+ years managing international projects including the design and construction of new manufacturing facilities; Area Development, "The New Rules of Industrial Site Selection" series, "Power Is the New Gatekeeper in Manufacturing Site Selection," https://www.areadevelopment.com/the-new-rules-of-industrial-site-selection/q2-2026/power-is-the-new-gatekeeper-in-manufacturing-site-selection.shtml; accessed 2026-08-14]
SUMMARY, not verbatim --- Segers frames this as a reversal in how manufacturers pick a site: the old sequence solved for the labor market and incentives first and confirmed infrastructure last, as a formality. That order has flipped. For large manufacturing projects today—auto plants, battery fabs, semiconductor facilities—the first question is no longer about workers or tax credits. It is about power. And the second question is whether that power can actually be delivered within the project timeline. SUMMARY, not verbatim --- She sizes the input that can sink a project outright: equipment delivery alone can outlast a manufacturer's production ramp schedule. Transformer lead times currently run between 18 and 36 months. For a manufacturer planning an aggressive ramp to production, that single constraint can be disqualifying. A site without a clear path to power—regardless of its other advantages—is a site that gets crossed off the list early. SUMMARY, not verbatim --- The piece reframes the competition itself: it is no longer manufacturer versus manufacturer for a site, it is manufacturer versus data center for the same finite grid capacity. Manufacturing projects are no longer competing only against other manufacturing projects. They are competing with data centers—which are proliferating rapidly, require enormous power loads, and are often prepared to move faster and pay more. In markets where grid capacity is limited, a data center can absorb available infrastructure before a manufacturer's project clears due diligence. SUMMARY, not verbatim --- The upshot is that readiness beats location: a community without a demonstrated utility hookup timeline does not lose to a better site, it loses to a quicker rival. Communities that understand this dynamic—and can demonstrate infrastructure readiness before they are needed—have a meaningful competitive advantage. Those that cannot often lose projects not to a better location, but to a faster one. SUMMARY, not verbatim --- Her conclusion inverts the old checklist order: infrastructure has to be confirmed first, before workforce or incentives, because a power failure voids everything else about the site. For manufacturers, the implication is direct: infrastructure evaluation cannot wait until late in the process. It has to be the first serious conversation—because if it fails, nothing else in the site profile matters. [Full passage: https://www.areadevelopment.com/… --- rehydrate before tournament use]
